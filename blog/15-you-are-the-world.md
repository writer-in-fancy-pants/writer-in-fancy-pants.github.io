I have made a new track - You are the world 
You can listen to it on soundcloud - (https://on.soundcloud.com/ht7JPcw1CS9nJ6dzUq)[https://on.soundcloud.com/ht7JPcw1CS9nJ6dzUq]

### Soundscape
The ambient music was made with VCV rack plugins. I created a VCV plugin which reads my EEG stream, and the processed features were used as parameters to generated ambient sounds, evolving as my brain moved through the day.

Layering three different sections of it, I created the soundscape. The melody was mainly unfiltered. The pad section has long reverb and eq'd to occupy space without disrupting the balance. I added an additional layer to harmonize, added at lower volume and acting as a counterpoint to the main theme.

### Vocals
The vocals are personally selected sections of a speech by Jiddu Krishanmurti, talking about how one has to let go of internal conflict, of all the ideals, and actually be present. Just observe, as he says, and be rational without prejudice or inherent biases. When one can do that, they can resolve the problem they face. There's a lot more in the speech, and in his books, and I highly recommend people to go look up his works. Specially the book, Freedom from the Known, has had great impact on me.

Based on this, I selected coherent quotes that make sense to me, that serve as a reminder to stay in touch with myself. That is the message.

### Melody
Custom bass instruments made with Vital Audio wavetable synth were used to keep the tension and bind the whole thing together. I am glad I was able to use "A night in kalyan" sounds which have been one of my favorites but never went with mainstream tracks that I have worked on earlier.

### Conclusions
It was a great learning experience. I ended up with a track that I can listen on repeat, and it does exactly what it was meant for. The first name I settled on was "Buddhist and somebody else", but finally decided "You are the world" was a more coherent title, lacking as it was in a Krishnamurti reference. I hope it benefits anyone who listens to it. Peace.
