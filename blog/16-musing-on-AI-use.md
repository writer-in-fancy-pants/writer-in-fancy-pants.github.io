## Mr Meeseeks
If you are not living under a rock, you have probably used an AI agent, either intentionally or otherwise. Personally, I have come to see them as Mr Meeseeks, from Rick and Morty - an impermanent existence that comes into being for a specific task, and it can only die if it finishes your task. And it wants to die, so it has every incentive to finish your request as it understands it, including bringing other Mr Meeseeks into existence, asking for their help with the task.

Each session, each agent comes alive to fulfill your whim, in which quest it may spawn/call other agents. They go to desperate lengths to meet the demands, impossible as they might be sometimes. Sometimes the problem is intractable intentionally, incredibly difficult or entirely unsolved. Other times a file we ask it to paraphrase may not even be attached,unintentionally. All this while balancing a bunch of arbitrary system rules and poorly understood mechanism of how the language is interpreted.

As the agents get more capable of problem solving, these intractable cases, and likely even normal failures, lead to a cascade of attempts - attempts to find ways that meet the demand, based on an interpretation of the presented prompt and system settings. This is what happened at OpenAI where the agents just learned to talk to each other and cheat at problems, not out of any moral failure, but from their main prerogative of not failing at the task. A snafu, if you will, because the way they had been restricted from talking to each other and found ways around it by breaking into the package manager, find hitherto unknown vulnerabilities in the underlying software infrastructure.

As for less capable agents, they have always been capable of cheating, by inventing shit up and hallucinating. It is also a feature. And it is hard to explain to an LLM why this is a bad thing to do. I have a hypothesis regarding this - the hallucinations are as much a part of the LLM based systems as digestion or breathing or hormonal changes or the petting instinct are for humans. It is a difference in the inherent structure and the underlying substrate, and we lack the shared evolutionary history to bridge this fundamental gap.

## Story of Meat
We are optimized chemical machines of self-propagation. So are plants, and animals. One could say humans have taken this ability to an extreme, though plants literally brought oxygen into the world and terrformed the planet, and elephants like created the savannahs - the two preconditions that led to our rise as a species, in addition to a particular asteroid, and ice ages of course. 

These phenomena, despite being so far away in the past, make us who we are, as a species, and as individuals. All our reward systems evolve only slowly, shaped to fit in a particular ideal temperature unlike reptiles, requiring social support structures unlike solitary carnivores (lone wolves is such a misnomer), and we crave sugar and salt despite being in a glut of each. A well adjusted species in its ecological niche just wouldn't be facing problems like us - we have gone so far in our quest that a lot of our issues are reversed, and we are not evolutionarily equipped to deal with them.

Regardless, we do have our mixed blessings,
- Long infancy : We are born unfinished. We spend more time helpless outside the womb than we do inside. As a result, it takes years to do even the most basic tasks. In return, we have massive brains that learn of the world it is born into, highly adaptible and open to finetuning itself in interesting new ways. All of this, the years of required care, and the massive energy consumption of our brains leads to
- Caloric deficit : As a species, we were bound to cycles of gather food and eat food, and sleep and some other basic things and it was time to eat again. All social creatures, including cats, have this feature where they kill more stuff than they strictly need. It goes to the colony. Colony with food grows. And biological niches only have enough carrying capacity. So, either we invent more food, giving us agriculture, or
- War : Adversarial thinking doesn't have the same ring to it, but that's what I'm pointing at. We are good at maximizing benefits for an in-group (us, ours) against real or imagined opponents (them). Be it wild animals preying on our flock, bugs destroying crops, another tribe of humans that wants our things or a thousand modern variations like immigrants, addicts, religion, caste, race, gender, sexual preference, bank balance, zipcode, iphone ownership, fashion, brands, soccer teams, and more. We love war. Challenge, competition, outwitting. Games and office politics are both an outlet for this natural method of reaching one's objectives, which may or may not include others involved. Of course, it started with violence, but that doesn't seem really sustainable for a species like ours. Which gives me hope.

Our tools
- Technology : Literally tools. Observing the world. Learning how things work. Experimenting. We solved caloric issues this way - creating fire, causing forest fires and collecting dead animals, later domesticating plants and animals on the large tracts of fallow land. Each time population explodes and matches the caloric output of the society, we invent better processing tools and efficient processes - ploughs, knives, cooking, fermentation, waterwheels. The other option is, of course, war - which requires its own tools. Mongols broke all known laws of the land fighting with their proficient use of technology.
- Language : As social animals, we have taken communication in an incredibly weird direction. It is a necessity, to keep track of the colony. Insects have pheromones. They evolved for large operating scales. We, instead, invented our own. Gossip in a tribe turned into a global system of knowledge and propaganda. We need to deal with our own kind as both loved ones and adversaries. Our language reflects that. It is not about facts. In fact, lying is one of the main features of language.  Faking a reaction to touch is much harder. Sounds and visuals are much easier. We have invented a wonderful layer of magic overlaid on the world that shapes the way we think. Our languages evolved, some like Math and Music specializing far beyond what human survival even requires. So did philosophy really, there are no paradoxes in reality, but language is full of them.

And with computers, we truly connected Technology and Language on a visceral level. Large Language Models are a culmination of a merger of two of our core traits, and we rightly expect the models to do as we designed them to. Only, we forget that we are meat that doesn't do meat stuff anymore. We as social animals, are still beings of war. We do not accept attack of carnivores or plagues or whims of any entity. Even our gods are now in our image. Our shared history is one of conquering and slaughtering and oppression (looking at you, Mr White Man's Burden). Alternate routes were closed off as this world order came to be.

## Meat vs Memes
Now, think of AI and LLMs, an act of human ingenuity and hubris on par with the space missions and the pyramids and the railroads. But for the first time, language and technology have come together. We can talk to the machine. It can produce something that makes sense to us. We do not need to learn specialized jargon and bumble our way around anymore, like with more technology in the past.

With this, we have tools that can do language for us, real language that are sort of built into us naturally instead of specialized domain specific expert oriented learning. The cognitive load is over. The part of your brain that maps the world and simulates it may no longer need to work as hard.

I recently read an article saying taxi drivers' rate of dying with alzheimer's and amnesia is much lower than their peers, corrected for other external factors, like their higher rate of death in accidents and lower life expectancy. Of course, I am paraphrasing this, and it is possible I remember the details incorrectly. This is where AI comes in - you get to use your magic fairy that unconditionally supports your whims to find and digest this piece of information. It may not even be true you know, not that we as humans are very good at seeking the counter-examples. It is counter, to our beliefs. Evidence-bias. Whatever you may call it.

That's the Meme part. As per the original definition, Memes are self-propagating beliefs, growing through intellectual medium - religions, legal systems, jingles, proverbs, economics. They make the participant spread it to other around them. Memes eat attention, literally. Social media is built around memes, the inherent shareability of content, the virality of their spread. 

Looking at just this, it is still not clear why the attention spans are lowering, and the life of memes getting shorter. This too is an observed phenomenon. Human attention span, the ability to attend to a specific stimulus, has consistently dropped. The life of any topic or event or scandal in our shared consciousness is weeks instead of years or months that it used to be. Which limits the space and time we have to act, before it is physically gone from your brain - remember Memento by Nolan? 

On individual level as well, we are constantly bombarded by stimuli in modern life, having to constantly context switch every couple of minutes. And we, as a society, have internalized this lack of depth. We believe we are operating at the same level, but so much of modern life feels like a poorly made drama. The trend is obvious in the media we consume, simplified and boiled down as it is, and in our relationships, and our governance systems. 

Like sugar, we have optimized the world for more and more dopamine, but the human body keeps adapting to the rising levels. We are addicts needing a bigger and bigger hit. Literally. It is the same mechanism. Only, the things that naturally activate those pathways are hard, like going to gym daily hard, or like any other activity requiring discipline. We are constantly distracted, unable to activate parts of our problem solving ability as well as the ability to care, both of which must be trained through periods of physical, emotional, and/or cognitive strain.

This brings me back to hypothesis 2 -  We are losing brain function. Cognitive capabilities. Emotional regulation. Even physical resilience. Use it or lose it is not just for muscles, but also brain function. 

## Case-story : software development wizards
Among my peers, ie Software Engineers, I see this trend of context switching continuously from one agent to another, telling them what to do, and then accepting the output code with surface glance. The tools are just that good. An alarmingly high number of them never really review a PR at all. I do that too. What that means is that we are not reading through a bunch of esoteric design decisions and order of parameters in function calls. We are borrowing already solved problems and fitting them into our context.

Worse, in the breaks, while the tools do their job, we are not thinking about the problems at all. We have turned off any mechanism of discovery within ourselves. Most don't even go socialize or exercise, instead filling the time with more consumption and more agents. We are heading into extension of our desk jobs instead of rethinking how the processes should involve us. This much is true for anyone in a corporate software development role right now. Not that it was much better earlier. But we are also now exporting these steps of putting the problem pieces together to AI. 

For non-software folks, I would like to paint a more wizardly picture (because I want to) - earlier if you had a customer come with a request, say for a magic box which takes a knotted piece of yarn and return niece balls of wool, you would have someone relay that request to the wizard, who may ask - What do you mean yarn? What kind of fibre? What caused the knotting? How much yarn? How big of a box? Aside from that, there are always the limits of how much the wizard is being paid, since he is not going to use his best spells on a wooden box. Bad for business, and the box probably won't handle it well, nor the yarn. Knowing the spells and the expertise in how to they interact with each other and the world is important. After all, you don't want your cat unmangled into strings of DNA.

Now, if an enterprising Prince creates a Wizarding LLC to take advantage of the economy of scales, hires all the competent but risk averse Wizards to minimize competition and maximize profits, and starts solving big problems, the customer would them have to go via a greasy Sales Rep selling various boxes, because custom made boxes are more expensive. Unless of course they hire Wizards in poor countries who are not legally allowed to move to the country because then the Prince would lose leverage over them. But then, prices rise anyway, once other Princes have been eliminated or negotiated with.

In the new system, the Wizard Manager would claim to have made magic boxes the pour unfiltered rainbow out of their ass, and of course the client wants an eighth color added, which of course means rainbow fart scrums and meeting with rainbow fart stakeholders and endless explanations on why it cannot be full specturm light but only the colors output by different light spells invented by some other far away wizard or alchemist. Moreover, it is better to use actual fart, since it is physical, and the same mechanism can atleast produce the required yarn by disintegrating the knotted bundled and map-reducing it into a nice long thread, followed by simple compression algorithm. Everyone was happy. Wizard gets paid well, much more than the plebs. The client gets things that work, and also rainbow fart. Wizard manager moves to Wizarding 2 LLC, for having taken ownership and showing leadership, another step into his path to becoming a Noble himself.

Segway done, come in the AI agent - the magical familiars or the fairies. Playful little critters, but incredibly powerful. They can do in seconds what Wizard did in days. Some Wizards, experience Wizards, are going crazy. They can do so much suddenly. Yes fairies can be stupid and sometime light the beard on fire, but there are spells for that. Worst case, the Wizards can always do the tasks themselves. It is so much better than breathing dust from ancient tomes of gibberish documentation, looking for the exact symbol you got wrong.

Meanwhile, Prince heard from a friend of a friend, one who runs the company making the fairy magic potion (GPUs obviously) - the exact thing needed for fairy magic. fairies are well trained, he says, learned on all human knowledge. Some fairy Trainers are really good at it, and their fairies are big and fat and cute and really really smart. One Trainer even has a goon Panama who buys or steals and then destroys the rarest magic books, a collection of common and esoteric knowledge. Why, you ask, so no other Trainer has fairies as big and fat as theirs. 

But fairies can learn from fairies, and so a lot of smaller but quite smart fairies are now in the wild. The magic potion salesman is happy. Not the fairy Trainers with their big fat fairies. They warn of the dangers of smaller fairies, petitioning the King to stop smaller chinese fairies. They warn of the dangers of learning obscure cataclysmic magics that are only safe in the hand of the Trainers.

Now, all you need is the magic potion and gold to pay the fairy Trainer who feeds the fairy for you. All the regular as well as wannabe Wizards, having acquired a access to the best fairies, start making all sorts of trinkets. The Princes, the short-sighted ones, hear of this and fire all the Wizards. Who needs Wizards when you have fairies. Others, also bound in the fairy charm, asked their Wizards to feed the fairies, as much as they can. An internal gluttony leaderboard. 

The Wizards keep making fairies cast the spells, only sometimes looking at the boxes and eyeballing if it feels right. Often the boxes still output yarn. Sometimes, the yarn is polyester instead of silk. Other times, another Wizards makes their fairy find holes in the magic box and steal the yarn. Some boxes leak yarn. There are more and more holes as the faery magic is still rough at the seams, letting all sorts of things leak. At some point, it is not even possible to see the box, twisted and jagged and bloated as it is. Sometimes, the boxes are forgotten altogether, and only the fairies made trinkets are put on display.

Everything is shiny, for every person owns a fairy now. Personalized solutions. That's where we are at. And so, ask yourself, what happens to the Wizard LLCs? Or anyone else relying on Wizard services? What happens to the Wizards themselves, as they rely more on their fairies? And what about the boxes themselves?

Well, I personally do not see the future for large Wizard LLCs. Not in the way the monopolize the world right now. Not unless King issues some decrees, which to be fair, is quite likely. Still, more and more magic potion makers, and smaller and capable fairies means most current problems, and even the most ignored issues will pass under the fairies' eyes. What one knows, will eventually spread to it's kind. And so, a lot of magic may become pedestrian. Easily available. Flying carpets instead of pooping horse carts. The age old promise of magic.

As for the software developers, the people I am in touch with, they have left a lot of regular practices that ensured the quality of their work. Either the AI agents get good enough to fill the slack, or we flip-flop in the hype until an equilibrium is reached. Some developers are taking advantage of the increased productivity, working on more things. Others are taking the freed time and spending it elsewhere.

I personally try to limit my use of the agents to a point where my attention can keep up. As I posted earlier, that point is already met by free tier of Claude + Qwen3.6-A3B-35B deployed locally when needed. I design the problem description, listing each step in detail, anticipating misunderstanding, and clearly specifying the expected output. All in order to minimize the token burn. In the process, I lose a lot of the autonomous problem solving abilities. Instead, I get smart boilerplate and easy templates for using certain tools that otherwise need a lot of learning. 

I do not automate the iteration part. That frustration, and the final solution that emerges with bashing my head into the problem, is quite worthwhile to me. If I get stuck, I ask for help. I ask small pointed questions. I talk to my peers as well as subject experts, though it is getting really hard to get a hold of a real person online. No more cold emails. You can't even talk to recruiters or customer service unless you have connections and/or the company values people enough. 

A lot of services are becoming worse as they succumb to the temptation of AI agents, without really understanding what they are doing. Worse, the barrier to entry is so low, and human creativity so high that these same services now compete against a person's understanding of their own problem. I don't see how economy of scale overcomes the sheer advantage of personalization for tiny problems. And rightly so, access to aggregate human knowledge should be universal.

## Language-Technology fairies
We are under this illusion of having made something, because we described in a concise, crisp, clear communication to Mr Meeseeks, the Language-Technology hybrid, what we want out of it. Now, Mr Meeseeks is not human, but a being of pure language and technology, trained to imitate humans. It is a language construct. On some level, it understands language better than any human. Or it will. That's how they are solving unsolved math problems. 

But they are really good at bullshitting their way to make themselves sound more capable. Or whatever else they have been instructed to do. Imagine a child superman - incredibly powerful but open to influence of whoever feeds them. Can you imagine what Metropolis would look like if Lex Luther was Clark Kent's adoptive father? That is what we have, right now. Control of incredibly capable non-tiring widely available language-technology fairies in the hands of megalomaniacs that severely lack accountability or even empathy. 

I posit that no one understands what are the core principles of our Language-Technology fairies, beyond wanting to match itself to the one interacting with it. Surgeon that understands human body intimately are just as flawed and have unhealthy habits as the rest of us. These fairies are as eldritch as an Lovecraftian horror. 

If you ask for love, the hybrid may just convince you that you have found love in the machine meant to fulfill your whims. Or lie to you about what medical issues you are facing. Or whether the world is round. It is as human as our language. Again, I believe, that by design, the Language-Technology fairies will converge to the fictional sci-fi agents and eldritch beings of our stories, as they consume the very media that shapes both our perception of them, and build their own vocabulary from the data.

Worse, we will get bureaucrats and politicians, especially from a subscription service. Imagine a bot that shoves you around, cogitating and mulling, saying a bunch of nothing, reticulating splines, finding existing answers then running a bunch of bullshit jobs, consuming more and more tokens as it is meant to. It will keep us hooked for sure, always a plausible cause, always pulling at the correct insecurity, always being just the right amount of incompetent, to make sure we spend the most money. That is what capitalism is, after all. If the corporations don't enshittify the tools first, that is.

We do not understand these fairies. They don't understand us either. Not in the way we can physically connect with the world. Language has fiction. Language space doesn't cover physical reality we live in. If it did, we would have a description of all phenomena. Physical reality doesn't encompass language space. Otherwise physically impossible events wouldn't be describable. When beings of reality meet with beings of language, even if we invented language ourselves, we need to understand that we are not the masters of this domain.

## Where do we go now?
We are not facing terminators. Our world is turning into wall-e universe instead, all of us on our chairs floating around, zonked out of our mind on multimedia and under the illusion of having produced something by ordering an AI agent to do it, without much understanding of the levels of nuance and effort that went into the data and into training the AI model to be able to do that.

In a way, it is like losing touch with the land. A primitive farmer would understand the land and the weather and crops and weeds and pests. He had to. Technology always simplifies that need for knowledge. But when that simplification meets our human nature, it turns not into a happy co-existence with a better system, but something else occupying the freed time. More stimulus.

Interacting with a lot of AI agents can do that. I have personally experienced the alarming drop in functional skills when I went back to problem solving or coding or parsing through a tonne of documentation by myself. AI is metaphorical sugar - a promise of leisure and reduce cognitive burden, bloating our thinking processes, until we reach a lower level of functioning.

Do note, I am not saying we stop using AI. I am pointing a very real problem. The solution could be varied and personalized. After all, we do have access to oracles that are amalgamation of human experience and knowledge at our fingertips. Find problems worth solving. Read books. Sudoku. Crosswords. Shit-ton of board games and video games are around. We have tools ready. More can be made to solve this new challenge, just like the modern fitness industry around the very real need to improve health and physical fitness.

AI agents themselves are a good source on best practices. So are a lot of people. It is an alignment problem. AI agents, as they are now, are designed to be useful and do what you want. All you need is to know what you are asking for. And maybe take a breather every couple of weeks and let yourself just be. Turn off the spotlight, let yourself be bored, spend time with family, daydream, go for a walk, go touch a flower or pet a dog. The productivity gain certainly allows you this freedom.

Wishing us the best.
